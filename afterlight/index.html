The gap

Digital life outlives us. Instructions rarely do.

Platforms offer one-off legacy contacts. Lawyers arrive late. Password managers hand over the keys without the context. Nothing holds the whole picture - or covers incapacity, not only death.

  • Platform silos Google, Apple, Facebook each have a different process. Your family shouldn't need a map of every vendor.
  • Lawyer lag Estate work is real and necessary - but slow, expensive, and silent on "close my Instagram" and "here's the letter for Maya."
  • Password dumps Emergency access without roles, wishes, or timing turns grief into a treasure hunt through every login you ever made.
  • Nothing for silence Most tools assume a death certificate. Real life includes hospital stays, lost phones, and long stretches of unreachable.
How it works

Circle. Wishes. Release.

Three moves. No feed. No gamification. Just a plan your people can actually follow.

01

Circle

Name a few stewards and give each a clear role - photos, accounts, wishes, or practical next steps.

02

Wishes

Write letters and inventory where things live. Store instructions, not raw passwords. Seal what should wait.

03

Release

Set how long silence can last, and require more than one person to confirm before sealed wishes unlock.

What it isn't

Built for stewardship, not surveillance.

Not a tracker

No location, no social feed, no daily guilt streaks. Presence is a quiet signal you choose to send.

Not a will

Afterlight does not replace estate documents or legal counsel. It holds the digital and human instructions lawyers rarely capture.

Not a password manager

Point to where credentials live and what to do with each account. Keep secrets in tools built for secrets.
